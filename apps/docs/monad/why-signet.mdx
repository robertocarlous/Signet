---
title: 'Why Signet, not your own registry'
description: 'What you get from a shared attestation layer that you lose by rolling your own contract'
---

You could write a `mapping(address => bool) verified` in an afternoon. Here is
what that costs you later, and what Signet gives you instead.

## A shared namespace

A Signet attestation is portable. A credential issued for your app is readable by
every other app on the same registry, with the same UID scheme and the same
events. Your own mapping is an island — every integrator has to learn your
bespoke shape, and nothing composes.

## Verifiers, not just issuers

The value of an attestation is how many places accept it. Signet's read side is
permissionless and already has tooling — SDK, CLI, indexer, a live verifier. A
private mapping starts with an audience of one.

## Delegation and passkeys, done once

- **EIP-712 delegated attest/revoke** — signer is offline, a relayer pays gas.
  Correct typed-data hashing, nonce and deadline replay protection, malleability
  checks. Easy to get subtly wrong.
- **WebAuthn / P-256 personhood** — `clientDataJSON` parsing, base64url challenge
  binding, low-s normalisation, RIP-7212 precompile use. This is the part judges
  and auditors look at hardest; Signet ships it verified and live.

## Resolver extensibility

Need per-attestation fees, an allowlist, a rate limit, a reward drop? Write a
resolver and point a schema at it. The core never changes, so your policy can't
break the primitive and vice-versa.

## Immutability as a feature

No admin key on the registries means no admin key to leak, phish, or govern.
Integrators can depend on the behaviour not changing under them.

## Who this is for

| Adopter | Why Signet |
| --- | --- |
| AI agent frameworks (ERC-8004) | a ready reputation / validation feed, not a bespoke store |
| AI content tools | portable, on-chain certificates of origin that survive platform migration |
| DeFi / RWA | reusable KYC / accreditation attestations instead of re-verifying per app |
| DAO & community tooling | skill and contribution credentials any app can check |
| Consumer apps needing "is this a real person" | one-tap passkey personhood, no wallet onboarding |

## When to roll your own

If the claim never leaves your database and no one else will ever verify it, a
mapping is fine. The moment a second party needs to trust it, you want a shared
layer.
